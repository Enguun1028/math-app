# Raw report 1: how students fall behind in math

Research agent output, kept for reference. Synthesized in `../left-behind-brief.md`.

Verification note from the agent: many primary PDFs (ERIC, APA, ed.gov) could not be fetched as text. Most claims were checked against abstracts, ERIC records and publisher pages, not full texts. Confidence tags reflect this.

## 1. How gaps accumulate

1.1 School-entry math is the strongest academic predictor of later achievement, ahead of reading and attention, controlling for cognition and family background (Duncan et al. 2007, 6 longitudinal datasets). [high] https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/dev-4361428.pdf , https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18020822/

1.2 Prior math is the main driver of later math; the effect of comprehensive math knowledge grows with age (Lin & Powell 2022, meta-analytic SEM, 250 studies, 580,437 students). [high for direction] https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/00346543211054576

1.3 Gaps persist, but prior knowledge does not predict normalized gains (Simonsmeier et al. 2022, 8,776 effect sizes): pretest-posttest stability r = .53, but prior knowledge vs normalized gain r ≈ −.06. Gaps persist unless targeted; once the missing prerequisite is supplied, a behind student can learn at a normal rate. [high] https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1326466

1.4 Kindergartners with persistent math difficulty had the lowest growth in grades 1-5; those who recovered did better but still grew more slowly (Morgan, Farkas & Wu 2009, ECLS-K). [high] https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219408331037

## 2. Breakpoint topics

2.1 Fraction knowledge at age 10 was the strongest of five math predictors of algebra and overall math at age 16; whole-number division second. Held after controlling for IQ, working memory, family income/education and other arithmetic (Siegler et al. 2012; UK BCS N = 3,677 and US PSID). [high] https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797612440101 , https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED552898.pdf

2.2 6th-grade fraction concepts predicted one-year math gains (β = .14, Bailey et al. 2012). Fraction magnitude predicted algebra readiness better than whole-number magnitude (Booth & Newton 2012). [high] https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/f/1681/files/2014/07/Bailey-et-al.-2012-fractions-JECP.pdf , https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ977998

2.3 National Math Advisory Panel (2008): fractions (including decimals, percents, negative fractions) "foundational for algebra" and "severely underdeveloped". Algebra I teachers (n = 743) reported poor preparation in fractions/decimals, order of operations, positive/negative integers, word problems and study habits. [high] https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED500486.pdf

2.4 Equals sign: many middle schoolers read "=" as "write the answer". About 30% of 5th graders have a relational view, about 80% by 9th grade; relational understanding predicted equation solving at every grade (Knuth et al. 2006; McNeil et al. 2006). [high] https://labweb.education.wisc.edu/knuth/taar/papers_rep_pub/JRME2006.pdf , https://cladlab.nd.edu/assets/250420/mcneiletal06.pdf

2.5 Proportional reasoning: students shift from overusing additive methods to overusing proportional methods. [medium-high] https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0885201420300046

2.6 Functions and graphs: persistent graph-as-picture and slope/height errors (Leinhardt et al. 1990; Clement 1985). [medium for prediction] https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543060001001 , http://people.umass.edu/clement/pdf/Misconceptions%20in%20Graphing.pdf

Ranking by predictive evidence: fractions > division > equals sign > integers > proportional reasoning > functions/graphs.

## 3. Misconceptions students recognize

- Fractions (whole-number bias, Lortie-Forgues, Tian & Siegler 2015): estimate 12/13 + 7/8 from 1, 2, 19, 21; only 24% of US 8th graders chose 2 in 1978, 27% in 2014. "1/3 + 1/4 = 2/7". "1/8 > 1/6 because 8 > 6". "Multiplying makes bigger, dividing makes smaller". [high] https://siegler.tc.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/2015-Lortie-Forgues-Tian-Siegler-facsimile.pdf
- Decimals: "longer is larger" (0.125 > 0.3): about 40% of Grade 5, about 5% by Year 10; also "shorter is larger" (Steinle & Stacey). [high] https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251804213_PERSISTENCE_OF_DECIMAL_MISCONCEPTIONS_AND_READINESS_TO_MOVE_TO_EXPERTISE
- Negatives: minus only as "subtract"; "−7 > −3"; "two negatives make a positive" everywhere (−3 − 5 = 8); 5 − (−2) vs 5 − 2. [medium-high] https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1124790.pdf
- Equals sign: answering 12 or 17 to 8 + 4 = □ + 5; run-on chains 3 + 4 = 7 + 2 = 9. [high]
- Variables: letter-as-object ("a = apples"), up to about 80% on diagnostic tasks (Küchemann, CSMS, ages 11-16); reversal error 6S = P, 37% of engineering freshmen (Clement 1982); 2x + 3 = 5x. [high] https://repository.uantwerpen.be/docstore/d:irua:30482 , https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ258398
- Proportion: additive strategy (2×3 photo to width 4 gives height 5); overuse (3 runners take 30 min). [medium-high]
- Graphs: graph-as-picture; higher line = steeper; slope as angle not rate; negative slope vs negative values. [high] http://people.umass.edu/clement/pdf/Misconceptions%20in%20Graphing.pdf , https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13138-025-00264-w

## 4. Procedural vs conceptual, and math language

4.1 Conceptual and procedural knowledge support each other iteratively; procedures without understanding are fragile (Rittle-Johnson, Schneider & Star 2015). [high] https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9302-x
4.2 Conceptual fraction knowledge predicted growth in fraction computation, word problems and estimation (Hecht & Vagi 2010, n = 181). [high] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3002259/
4.3 Math vocabulary correlates with math performance r ≈ .49 (r ≈ .17-.41 after controls), stronger for secondary math (Lin, Peng & Zeng 2021, 40 studies). [high] https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344194571_Understanding_the_Relation_Between_Mathematics_Vocabulary_and_Mathematics_Performance_A_Meta-analysis
4.4 EEF KS2/3 guidance: plan for misconceptions; they are "met-befores" that over-generalize ideas that worked before. [high] https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/maths-ks-2-3

## 5. When the gap forms and the late-student profile

5.1 Bottom-quartile kindergarten entrants grew least through grade 3 (Bodovski & Farkas 2007); evidence mixed across studies. [medium-high] https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608014001381
5.2 About 28% of OECD 15-year-olds below baseline in at least one subject; low performers disproportionately disadvantaged; absenteeism, low persistence, low math self-confidence contribute (OECD 2016). [high] https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/low-performing-students_9789264250246-en.html
5.3 Typical behind student at 13-17 [medium]: adequate addition/subtraction, weak multiplication facts, fragile long division; fractions/decimals/percent by half-remembered rules; integers by rote sign rules; "=" as "write the answer"; letters as labels; poor vocabulary; rigid procedures that break in word problems; low confidence. Several linked gaps, not one.

## Implications (agent's list)

- Diagnose before teaching; walk backward along prerequisite edges. Once fixed, normal-rate learning is possible (motivating).
- Misconception-revealing distractors route to the prerequisite node.
- Each node has a concept check and a procedure check; mastered only when both pass.
- Vocabulary as a prerequisite layer: 3-6 key terms per node with Mongolian term, gloss, picture, example.
- Magnitude and the number line as the thread through place value, decimals, fractions, negatives, percent.
- Equals sign and meaning of a variable as their own small nodes or gates before linear equations.
- Short, low-stakes, encouraging sessions; going back framed as normal.
- Allow working on a higher topic while patching a lower one.

## Highest-leverage topics (agent's ranking)

1. Fractions. 2. Division / divisibility, GCD, LCM. 3. Place value to decimals. 4. Negative numbers. 5. Variables/expressions plus the equals sign. 6. Ratio/proportion and percent. 7. Linear functions (slope, graphs). Later topics mostly fail because of these.
