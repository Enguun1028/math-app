# Raw report 3: the teenager side and what works for catch-up

Research agent output, kept for reference. Synthesized in `../left-behind-brief.md`.

Verification note from the agent: search was rate-limited at times. The Botswana NBER paper, Kraft et al. 2024 and Sammallahti 2023 were read in full; everything else from abstracts and search summaries.

## 1. Math anxiety and identity

1.1 Prevalence [high]. PISA 2022 OECD average for 15-year-olds: 65% worry about poor marks, 55% worry about failing, 40% feel nervous or helpless solving problems. Anxiety is linked to lower scores in every system after controls. Less anxiety where teachers are seen as supportive. Mongolia: 49% reached Level 2 (OECD 69%), mean 425.
https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-v_c2e44201-en.html ; https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-i-and-ii-country-notes_ed6fbcc5-en/mongolia_a93e1396-en.html ; PISA 2012: https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2015/02/does-math-make-you-anxious_g17a25bf/5js6b2579tnx-en.pdf

1.2 Avoidance loop [medium-high]. Gaps and anxiety reinforce each other. Choe et al. 2019: anxious people choose easy, low-reward math problems over hard, high-reward ones (not for word problems). Reduce the perceived cost of the first step.
https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aay1062

1.3 Help-seeking shame [medium-high]. Ryan, Pintrich & Midgley (7th-8th graders): low achievers avoid asking for help most because needing help signals incapability. A private app lowers this.
https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/44450

1.4 What reduces anxiety [medium]. Sammallahti et al. 2023 (50 studies, 75 effect sizes): g = −0.47 on anxiety, +0.50 on performance; skill-building and emotion regulation both work; larger effects for longer interventions and students over 12. Balt et al. 2022: formal math programs and educational games reduce anxiety.
https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1400784.pdf ; https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.798516/full

1.5 Expressive writing unreliable [low]. Early lab results did not replicate (Scheibe et al. n = 168; large replication near zero).
https://escholarship.org/uc/item/7378s5s2

1.6 Growth mindset mixed [medium]. Sisk et al. 2018: d = 0.08. Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023: less benefit in higher-quality studies, bias signs. Yeager et al. 2019 (about 12,500 9th graders): +0.10 GPA for below-median students where peer norms supportive.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1466-y ; https://artscimedia.case.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/141/2020/06/26110416/Macnamara-Burgoyne-2023.pdf

## 2. Motivating older students

2.1 Utility value [medium-high]. Hulleman & Harackiewicz 2009 (262 high-school students): writing how material relates to one's life raised interest and grades, mainly for students with low expectations.
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232580960_Enhancing_Interest_and_Performance_With_a_Utility_Value_Intervention

2.2 Autonomy and competence [medium, theory-based]. Frame review as unlocking the grade-level goal; hide grade labels on foundational topics; let students choose their route.

2.3 Visible progress [medium]. EEF: feedback +6 months, metacognition +7 to +8 months. Feedback on task and process, not ability.
https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/feedback

## 3. Catch-up interventions

3.1 Tutoring [high]. Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan (96 RCTs): 0.37 SD. Kraft, Schueler & Falken (265 RCTs): effects shrink with scale (under 100 students 0.44; 100-399 0.30; 400-999 0.21; over 1,000 0.16 SD). High-school math tutoring 0.55 SD but only 13 estimates.
https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476 ; https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-1031

3.2 Saga/Match, Chicago 9th-10th graders [high]. Study 1: 0.09 ITT, 0.18 participants; Study 2: 0.14 ITT, 0.40 participants. Math course failures fell about half. Daily 2:1 tutoring plus adaptive software. Title: "Not Too Late".
https://www.nber.org/papers/w28531 ; https://www.povertyactionlab.org/case-study/individualized-tutoring-improve-learning

3.3 Teaching at the Right Level [high]. Simple assessment, group by level not grade, teach foundations. Six RCTs in India; 10-20 day camps gained 0.7-1.0 levels. Fails without dedicated time or a clear level tool.
https://www.povertyactionlab.org/case-study/teaching-right-level-improve-learning ; https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22746/w22746.pdf

3.4 Mindspark, India grades 6-9 [high]. +0.37 SD math in 4.5 months; similar absolute gains, so largest relative gains for the weakest. Closest analogue to our app.
https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20171112

3.5 Mastery learning [medium-high]. EEF +5 months overall, +6 in math/science; high move-on threshold and quick corrective help.
https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/mastery-learning

3.6 Formative assessment [medium]. Kingston & Nash 2011: about 0.20 overall, 0.17 in math; computer-based about 0.28. ASSISTments RCT (2,850 7th graders): g = 0.18, instant feedback plus daily teacher reports.
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1745-3992.2011.00220.x ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2332858416673968

3.7 Interleaved retrieval [high]. Rohrer et al. 2020 (787 7th graders): 61% vs 38% on delayed test, d = 0.83.
https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1237752

3.8 Worked examples [high]. Barbieri et al. 2023 (55 studies): g = 0.48; stronger with self-explanation; incorrect examples help.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1

3.9 Accelerate, don't remediate [medium-low]. TNTP/Zearn 2021: grade-level work with just-in-time prerequisites vs remediation; 27% more grade-level lessons completed (49% for students of color and low-income). Correlational, elementary, single platform.
https://tntp.org/publication/accelerate-dont-remediate/

3.10 Hattie not used as a primary source.

## 4. Self-directed learning on phones

4.1 The 5% problem [medium-high]. Khan, DreamBox, i-Ready, IXL: 0.14-0.26 SD at recommended dose, but only about 5% reach it (4.7% for Khan's 30 min/week), skewed toward stronger students. Khan 2022-23 (350,000 students): 9% hit 30+ min/week (ES about 0.36). Correlational.
https://www.educationnext.org/5-percent-problem-online-mathematics-programs-may-benefit-most-kids-who-need-it-least/ ; https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024/

4.2 Botswana Young 1ove phone tutoring (4,500 families, primary) [high for primary, low transfer]. SMS alone: no effect. SMS plus weekly 15-20 min call: +0.121 SD (0.167 full participants), 31% less innumeracy; gains transferred to fractions; 0.63-0.89 SD per $100.
https://www.nber.org/papers/w28205 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-022-01381-z ; https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/limiting-learning-loss-using-phone-based-programming-during-covid-19-botswana

4.3 Little rigorous evidence for fully solo teen phone learning in low- and middle-income countries [low].

## 5. Teachers and tutors using gap reports

- Work when tied to a routine action (ASSISTments daily review, TaRL level tool). [medium]
- Reports alone change little (IES data-use evidence minimal; Bihar TaRL failed without time). [medium]
- Scalable tutoring bundles consistent tutor, set schedule, aligned materials. [medium]

## Design implications (agent's list)

1. Short low-stakes diagnostic, 5-10 items stepping down from a grade-level goal; "your starting point", never a score or grade.
2. Gaps as unlocks: "To reach Linear equations, strengthen these 2 foundations." No grade labels on foundations.
3. Accelerate: anchor to the school topic needed now; pull in prerequisites just in time.
4. "Learned" needs evidence: 2-3 correct retrieval items, spaced re-checks (1 day, 1 week, 1 month), interleaved; gentle fading; never mark failed.
5. Tiny fast first win; worked example plus self-explanation before practice.
6. Private help; no visible help counters.
7. Progress over time, no peer comparison.
8. Light relevance prompt at topic start.
9. Plan for human contact (teacher/tutor check-ins).
10. Measure: weekly minutes and share reaching dose, week-1/week-4 retention, verified mastery and its durability, grade-level topics unlocked, monthly 3-item confidence pulse, whether teachers act. Teacher report: one screen, who is stuck and on which prerequisite, grouped by gap.

## Things to avoid (agent's list)

Standalone mindset slogans; grade-level or remedial labels; forcing all below-grade work first; self-reported checkmarks with no check; automated content or push alone; timed, high-stakes or public quizzes, leaderboards, streak-loss punishment, visible help counters; treating expressive writing or Hattie as proven; raw dashboards without a suggested action; assuming pilot effects survive at scale.
