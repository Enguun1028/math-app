## Not verified or left out
- MacTutor has no biography of Minggatu (every URL tried returned 404). The book's completion year (often given as 1774) was not confirmed, so the story leaves the date out.
- The Mongolian names for large numbers (түм, бум, тэрбум...) come from a Wikipedia table that is marked unreferenced. That slide is marked draft. The claim about Rolbiidorj and 10⁶⁶ is uncited and was left out.
- No source was found for how Mongolia used the abacus (сампин) historically or in schools. The slides only say what a suanpan is.
- The Hippasus legend about √2 and the Laplace quote about logarithms have no reliable source, so they were left out.
- Mongolia's 1964 IMO team rank of 8 looks inconsistent on imo-official.org, so the story uses 2021 (rank 11, 90.6%) instead. Gold medallists are not named, because the Cyrillic spelling of their names is uncertain. imo-official.org lists them in Latin script: 1997 Ulziibat Battulga, 2012 Turbat Enkhbayar, 2019 and 2021 Unubold Munkhbat, 2020 Misheel Otgonbayar, 2021 Nyamdavaa Amar, 2023 and 2024 Enerelt Delgerdalai, 2025 Iveel Arvis.
- Mongolia has no modern mathematicians beyond L. Shagdar from a reliable English source. A Mongolian-language source (the Academy of Sciences or the National University of Mongolia) is needed for more names.
- The π record (314 trillion digits, Nov 2025) may have been broken since.
